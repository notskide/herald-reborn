# Copyright, Data Licensing, and AI

Public accessibility does not automatically grant permission to copy, redistribute, train on, or republish content. Copyright, contract terms, database rights, privacy law, and license compatibility may matter depending on the source and jurisdiction.

Separate these questions: may the system access the source; may it process the content for the planned purpose; may copies, embeddings, or passages be retained; may content be redistributed; what attribution is required; and how is deletion handled if permission changes?

A knowledge base can often use original explanations, concise summaries, claims linked to sources, and provenance records instead of copying long passages. Paraphrasing is not a universal legal exemption. Record source URL, publisher, access date, known license, and intended use. Prefer official documentation, openly licensed material, public-domain works, or content with explicit reuse permissions. Preserve required notices.

An embedding is not automatically legally or operationally risk-free because it is less readable than the original prose. Fair use and similar exceptions depend on specific facts and jurisdiction; they are not blanket permission for every AI use. Consult qualified legal advice when the issue is material.
